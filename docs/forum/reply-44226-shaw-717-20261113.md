You are right, and Snapmaker has confirmed it. This is issue #717 on GitHub, "Gradient is just 50/50 mix", open since August: https://github.com/Snapmaker/OrcaSlicer/issues/717

Their answer there: gradient mode only takes effect with "Subdivide Mix Layer" and "Full domain" turned on by hand (Filament tab), and the plan is to turn them on automatically in a future release.

That matches what I measured on my cube, where I asked for 80% of filament 1 at the bottom and 20% at the top:

| settings | share of filament 1, bottom to top |
|---|---|
| defaults | 57, 50, 50, 50, 44 % |
| "Subdivide Mix Layer" + "Full domain" | 65, 56, 50, 44, 38 % |
| the same + "Apply subdivision to infill" | 78, 62, 50, 38, 25 % |

The first row is your green: 1:1 all the way up. So for blue to yellow I would turn on all three and slice again. The preview will still show both colours on every layer; the gradient is in how thick each one is.

Two things from that GitHub thread that I could not check myself. The staff reply also says the mixed filament has to be painted onto the model; my cube had it assigned to the whole object and still ramped once "Full domain" was on. And users there report that a gradient created with the program in Spanish or German turns into a plain 50/50 mix, which Snapmaker says is fixed in an upcoming release. If your Orca is not in English and the three options change nothing, creating the gradient again with the language set to English is worth one try.

Nothing here was printed; the numbers come from the G-code of a 20 mm cube sliced with Snapmaker Orca 2.4.1.
